Entendimiento de Datos

Exploración y análisis del dataset VIIRS/SUOMI-NPP · Cundinamarca, Colombia

Objetivos del Negocio

Análisis ambiental de contaminación lumínica utilizando datos satelitales VIIRS en Cundinamarca.

Light pollution has become an increasing environmental problem in urban and semi-urban areas due to the excessive and uncontrolled use of artificial lighting during nighttime. High levels of light pollution can negatively affect ecosystems, human health, energy consumption, and the visibility of the night sky.

In Cundinamarca, the growth of urban areas and nighttime infrastructure has generated significant variations in light intensity between municipalities and populated sectors. However, identifying and analyzing these areas manually is difficult because of the large amount of geographic and environmental information involved.

The purpose of this project is to analyze nighttime light pollution levels in Cundinamarca using open satellite data in order to identify areas with low, medium, and high levels of artificial illumination. Through the analysis of satellite information, the project seeks to provide a better understanding of how light pollution is distributed geographically across the region.

This project aims to support environmental analysis and territorial studies by generating organized and interpretable information that can help identify critical areas affected by excessive nighttime lighting.

Key Questions

The project seeks to answer the following questions:

1. Which areas of Cundinamarca present the highest levels of nighttime light pollution?

2. Is it possible to identify patterns of artificial lighting using satellite data?

3. How are light intensity levels geographically distributed across the region?

4. Can satellite radiance values be used to classify pollution levels effectively?

5. What areas present low, medium, or high concentrations of nighttime artificial light?

Data Evaluation

The project uses open satellite data related to nighttime light intensity collected from the VIIRS Nighttime Lights program. These data provide information about the amount of artificial illumination detected in different geographic locations during nighttime.

The available data include geographic coordinates and radiance intensity values that represent the level of light emitted in specific areas.

The dataset is considered appropriate for the project because:

1. It comes from reliable scientific and official sources.

2. It contains environmental and geographic information relevant to the problem.

3. The data allow the identification of variations in nighttime light intensity.

4. The information is structured and suitable for analysis.

Additionally, the dataset provides enough information to identify potential differences between urban and rural areas in terms of artificial lighting concentration.

Before analysis, the data may require cleaning and validation processes to ensure consistency and reliability.

Feasibility Analysis

Technical Feasibility

The project is technically feasible because open satellite data related to nighttime illumination are publicly available and accessible for analysis.

The information obtained provides measurable radiance values that can be used to study variations in light pollution across geographic areas. Furthermore, the available data contain sufficient records to support environmental classification and analysis processes.

The project does not require specialized hardware infrastructure because the analysis can be performed using standard computational resources.

Organizational Feasibility

The project is viable in an academic and research context because it is based on publicly available environmental data and focuses on the analysis of a current environmental issue.

In addition, the study can contribute to environmental awareness and provide useful information for future urban and territorial studies related to sustainable development and environmental management.

Risks and Limitations

Some limitations and risks associated with the project include:

1. Variations in satellite measurements over time.

2. Possible inconsistencies in geographic coverage.

3. Dependence on the quality and availability of satellite data.

4. Difficulty in validating certain environmental conditions directly on-site.

5. Limitations in representing all environmental factors associated with light pollution.

Despite these limitations, the available data provide sufficient information to carry out a meaningful environmental analysis of nighttime light pollution in Cundinamarca.
