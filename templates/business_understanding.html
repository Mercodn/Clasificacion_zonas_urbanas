Business Understanding

Define the problem, business objectives, feasibility, risks, and expected impact of the light pollution classification project.

Problem Context

Artificial nighttime lighting is a growing environmental concern affecting ecosystems, astronomy, and human health. This project analyzes nighttime light pollution in Cundinamarca, Colombia using VIIRS/SUOMI-NPP satellite radiance data to classify zones and enable evidence-based environmental monitoring.

Business Objectives

  • Quantify: Measure and map nighttime radiance distribution across Cundinamarca.
  • Classify: Assign each location to a light pollution zone (six categories from pristine to metropolitan).
  • Enable: Provide a scalable prediction API for future monitoring and decision-making.

Key Business Questions

  • How does light pollution vary across urban, suburban, and rural zones in Cundinamarca?
  • Which areas have pristine night skies suitable for astronomical observation?
  • Can we accurately classify new locations based on radiance alone?

    Project Feasibility

    • ✓ Data Available: 8,360+ VIIRS observations with complete radiance values.
    • ✓ Technical Feasibility: Standard ML classification techniques applicable.
    • ✓ Implementation Timeline: 3-phase CRISP-ML approach achievable in academic timeframe.

Risks & Limitations

  • Data: Radiance alone may not capture all nuances; cloud cover can bias observations.
  • Model: Classification rules are threshold-based; boundary cases may be ambiguous.
  • Scope: Analysis limited to Cundinamarca; generalization to other regions untested.

Expected Impact

  • Environmental: Baseline for light pollution monitoring and sky quality assessment.
  • Urban Planning: Data-driven insights for sustainable urban design.
  • Science: Resource for astronomers identifying optimal observation sites.
  • Success Metric: Accuracy ≥ 85% on test set; interpretable zone assignments.
