1. BUSINESS OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

1.1 Problem Statement
---------------------
Light pollution has become an increasing environmental problem in urban and 
semi-urban areas due to the excessive and uncontrolled use of artificial 
lighting during nighttime. High levels of light pollution can negatively affect:
    • Ecosystems (disruption of nocturnal wildlife behavior)
    • Human health (circadian rhythm disruption, sleep disorders)
    • Energy consumption (inefficient lighting practices)
    • Visibility of the night sky (astronomical observation impact)

In Cundinamarca, the growth of urban areas and nighttime infrastructure has 
generated significant variations in light intensity between municipalities and 
populated sectors. Identifying and analyzing these areas manually is difficult 
because of the large amount of geographic and environmental information involved.

1.2 Project Goal
----------------
Analyze and classify nighttime light pollution levels in Cundinamarca using 
open satellite data in order to:
    • Identify areas with low, medium, and high levels of artificial illumination
    • Support environmental analysis and territorial studies
    • Generate organized and interpretable information for stakeholders
    • Enable data-driven decision-making for urban planning and environmental management

1.3 Project Objectives (SMART)
-------------------------------
    a) Develop a classification model that categorizes geographic zones in 
       Cundinamarca into light pollution levels (Low/Medium/High) with ≥85% accuracy
    
    b) Process and prepare VIIRS nighttime lights data to create a usable dataset 
       containing ≥8000 point observations with radiance and location information
    
    c) Identify geographic patterns and hotspots of light pollution across 
       municipalities and populated sectors in Cundinamarca
    
    d) Create a web-based visualization interface enabling stakeholders to explore 
       and interpret light pollution patterns interactively

1.4 Stakeholders and Users
---------------------------
    • Primary: Environmental agencies and city planners in Cundinamarca
    • Secondary: Academic researchers studying urban sustainability
    • Tertiary: Policymakers developing light pollution regulations
    • Technical: Data science and GIS teams conducting territorial analysis

1.5 Expected Business Value
----------------------------
    • Data-driven insights for urban development planning
    • Evidence base for environmental policy creation
    • Cost reduction through optimization of public lighting infrastructure
    • Foundation for future environmental monitoring systems
    • Academic contribution to environmental science in the region

3. DATA UNDERSTANDING AND EVALUATION
================================================================================

3.1 Data Sources
----------------

Primary Data Source: VIIRS Nighttime Lights
    • Provider: NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration)
    • Satellite Program: VIIRS DNB (Day/Night Band) on Suomi NPP and NOAA-20
    • Product: Monthly composites of nighttime radiance
    • Availability: Public and free to access
    • URL: https://earthdata.nasa.gov/ or Google Earth Engine

Data Characteristics:
    • Geographic Coverage: Global; subset extracted for Cundinamarca region
    • Spatial Resolution: ~750m per pixel at nadir
    • Temporal Resolution: Monthly and annual composites available
    • Time Period Available: 2012 onwards (continuous)
    • Measurement Unit: Radiance (nanoWatts/cm²/sr)
    • Format: GeoTIFF, NetCDF, or point shapefile

Metadata included:
    • Longitude and Latitude (geographic coordinates)
    • Radiance intensity (avg_rad: average radiance value per area)
    • Quality flags (indicating data reliability)
    • GeoJSON format for spatial representation

3.2 Supplementary Data Sources
-------------------------------

To enrich the analysis, the project incorporates:

    a) Balanced Polygon Dataset (balanced_dataSet_more_pollutants.csv)
       - Source: External environmental study
       - Contains: Polygon geometries representing administrative/geographic areas
       - Variables: 
         * ln_mean_NTL, ln_sum_NTL (logarithmic NTL statistics)
         * mean_PM25 (particulate matter - air pollution proxy)
         * mean_NO2, NO2_std (nitrogen dioxide measurements)
         * ln_GDPpc (logged GDP per capita - economic indicator)
         * Other socio-economic and environmental indicators
       - Purpose: Validate NTL-to-radiance proxy and enrich feature set
       - Coverage: Geographic zones in Cundinamarca

3.3 Data Evaluation
--------------------

Data Quality Assessment:
    • Reliability: VIIRS is operated by NOAA, a trusted scientific organization
    • Relevance: Directly measures light emission, core to light pollution analysis
    • Completeness: Provides coverage across Cundinamarca with acceptable spatial resolution
    • Consistency: Monthly composites ensure standardized, comparable measurements
    • Temporal Coverage: Multiple years allow trend analysis and pattern validation

Suitability for Project:
    ✓ Contains measurable radiance values suitable for quantitative classification
    ✓ Sufficient spatial resolution to distinguish urban vs. rural areas
    ✓ Sufficient data volume (8360+ points) to support ML model training
    ✓ Open and freely accessible, ensuring reproducibility
    ✓ Well-documented and widely used in environmental research

Data Constraints & Considerations:
    • Spatial Resolution Limitation: 750m pixels may not capture fine-grained 
      variations within cities
    • Temporal Granularity: Monthly composites may obscure short-term variations
    • Atmospheric Effects: Cloud cover and aerosols can affect radiance measurements
    • Bloom Effect: Bright urban lights can bleed into surrounding pixels
    • No Direct Validation Data: Ground truth light pollution measurements are 
      limited for direct accuracy validation

5. RISKS AND LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

5.1 Data Quality Risks
----------------------

Risk: Variations in Satellite Measurements Over Time
    Probability: High
    Impact: Model training accuracy affected
    Mitigation: Use multiple years of data, apply temporal normalization,
                validate model stability across time periods

Risk: Atmospheric Interference (clouds, aerosols)
    Probability: High (seasonal)
    Impact: Biased radiance measurements
    Mitigation: Use quality flags to filter low-quality measurements,
                aggregate seasonal variations, test model robustness

Risk: Data Gaps in Coverage
    Probability: Medium
    Impact: Incomplete geographic representation
    Mitigation: Document coverage gaps, interpolate where appropriate,
                acknowledge limitations in final analysis

5.2 Model and Analysis Risks
-----------------------------

Risk: Insufficient Data Variability
    Probability: Low
    Impact: Model fails to learn meaningful patterns
    Mitigation: Preliminary exploratory analysis (done); 8360+ points with 
                wide radiance range confirm variability exists

Risk: Class Imbalance (unequal distribution of Low/Medium/High zones)
    Probability: Medium
    Impact: Model biased toward majority class
    Mitigation: Use stratified cross-validation, class weights in training,
                SMOTE or undersampling if needed

Risk: Overfitting to Local Patterns
    Probability: Medium
    Impact: Poor generalization to unseen areas
    Mitigation: Regularization in model, cross-validation, hold-out test set,
                spatial validation (geographic partitioning)

5.3 Technical Limitations
--------------------------

Spatial Resolution Limitation:
    • VIIRS 750m pixels may not capture sub-neighborhood variations
    • Urban features smaller than pixel size are smoothed
    • Mitigation: Document resolution in results; acknowledge limited detail

Temporal Granularity:
    • Monthly composites hide day-to-day or week-to-week variation
    • Seasonal effects may confound patterns
    • Mitigation: Use annual composites; analyze seasonal patterns separately

Validation Challenge:
    • No direct on-site light pollution measurements for validation
    • Ground truth data would require expensive light meter surveys
    • Mitigation: Use domain expertise and literature comparison for plausibility

5.4 Project Scope Limitations
-----------------------------

Geographic Scope:
    • Project focuses only on Cundinamarca; results not generalizable to other regions
    • Urban-rural patterns may differ in other Colombian regions

Temporal Scope:
    • Analysis based on data available; historical trends limited by VIIRS start date (2012)
    • Future projections not within scope

Feature Scope:
    • Analysis focuses on radiance; does not account for all light pollution factors
    • Spectral composition, light spectrum, and chromatic effects not measured
    • Human perception and ecological impact not directly quantified

Model Scope:
    • Three-class classification (Low/Medium/High) is simplification
    • Continuous pollution index might be more informative
    • Time series modeling not in scope for Phase 1

5.5 Mitigation Strategy Summary
--------------------------------

Critical Risks Addressed:
    1. Data gaps → Quality filtering + documentation
    2. Model performance → Rigorous cross-validation + ensemble approach
    3. Validation difficulty → Domain literature comparison + stakeholder review
    4. Overfitting → Regularization + geographic cross-validation

Residual Risks Accepted:
    • Spatial resolution limitation (acceptable for regional analysis)
    • Temporal granularity (standard for public satellite data)
    • Three-class simplification (pragmatic balance between detail and usability)
